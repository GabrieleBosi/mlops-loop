## How each failure was attributed

Each sampled row is charged to the first component in pipeline order that could have prevented it. The rules are code, in `mlops_loop/analysis.py`, so the tally is reproducible rather than a judgement call:

1. **split**: the row is outside the distribution the champion was fitted on. A categorical value never appears in its training rows, or a numeric feature sits beyond the range they cover. The comparison is against the rows this champion actually saw (`train+fibre-monthly`), not against the train split, because a challenger promoted by `drift` was fitted on more than train.
2. **features**: the row sits where the feature set cannot separate anything. Its 25 nearest training neighbours in the encoded space churn at within 0.05 of the training base rate, so there is no signal to read.
3. **model**: in distribution, informative neighbourhood, and the model still ranked it wrongly. No choice of threshold would fix it.
4. **threshold**: ranked correctly, only the cut is wrong. It is right at the validation-optimal threshold (0.5745) and wrong at 0.5000.

## The biggest cell

**model**, 17 of 20 sampled failures (85%).

17 of 20 sampled failures (85%) are rows in distribution whose neighbourhoods do carry signal, and the model still ranked them wrongly. They are not near misses. Of the 188 such failures across the whole holdout, 140 are false positives at a median predicted probability of 0.739: the model says these customers are leaving and they stay. No threshold reaches them, because moving the cut far enough to catch them would surrender most of the true positives too. The shape of it is over-prediction. The champion flags 36.9% of the holdout as churners against an actual rate of 26.5%, which is what `class_weight='balanced'` does to a logistic regression: it buys recall by shifting every probability up, and PR-AUC, the metric that selected it, cannot see the cost because it only reads the ranking. Session 2 saw the same thing from the other end, when this model cleared the gate's Brier line by 0.0019.

## The cheapest fix

Put calibration into the selection rule, and let the sweep decide again. The cheapest version is two lines of config and no new code: add Brier to `configs/sweep.yaml` as a constraint alongside the PR-AUC objective, so a config that ranks a hair better and calibrates much worse cannot win, and re-run `python -m mlops_loop train`. The sweep already contains the unweighted twin of the current champion, so the alternative is measured, not hypothetical: in Session 2 `lr-c0.05` scored val PR-AUC 0.5731 against the champion's 0.5851 and val Brier 0.1604 against 0.1868. That is 0.012 of ranking traded for 0.026 of calibration. It costs 26 seconds of compute and it is falsifiable: if the tally after the change still puts most failures in `model`, the diagnosis was wrong and the next thing to try is capacity or features, in that order. What is not cheap, and not justified by this table, is adding a calibration layer or a new model family before the free experiment has been run.

Not applied in this session. The brief asks for error analysis before the fix, and a fix chosen and applied in the same breath is a fix nobody measured.
